# SIESTA — Exhaustive Technical Review

**Spanish Initiative for Electronic Simulations with Thousands of Atoms**

---

## 1. Overview

SIESTA is a free, open-source software package and an associated first-principles *method* for electronic-structure calculations and *ab initio* molecular dynamics simulations of molecules and solids, based on Density Functional Theory (DFT). It was the first widely used code to implement a fully self-consistent, linear-scaling (order-*N*, O(*N*)) approach to DFT, and it remains one of the most cited and most flexible members of the localized-basis-set family of electronic-structure codes.

- **Initial release:** 1996
- **Current stable release:** 5.4.1 (16 September 2025)
- **License:** GNU General Public License v3 (GPLv3) — fully open source since the 4.0 release (13 May 2016); prior to that, SIESTA was free for academic use but closed-source
- **Language:** Fortran (with C for some components)
- **Repository / development:** GitLab (`gitlab.com/siesta-project/siesta`), previously hosted on Launchpad
- **Homepage:** siesta-project.org
- **Documentation:** docs.siesta-project.org

SIESTA is developed and maintained by a distributed international collaboration of academic groups, historically centered on institutions in Spain (ICMAB-CSIC, ICN2, Universidad de Cantabria, Universidad Autónoma de Madrid, DIPC, CFM, Nanogune) together with international partners (DTU Denmark, Duke University, University of Oviedo, Curtin University, Charles University Prague, and others), and participates in pan-European infrastructure efforts such as the MaX (Materials at the Exascale) Centre of Excellence and the Electronic Structure Library (ESL).

## 2. Core Methodology

### 2.1 Fundamental ingredients

SIESTA's method rests on three principal pillars:

1. **Numerical atomic orbitals (NAOs) as basis functions** — strictly localized (finite-support), atom-centered basis functions used to expand the Kohn–Sham (KS) wavefunctions, in the spirit of the linear combination of atomic orbitals (LCAO) approach.
2. **Norm-conserving pseudopotentials** — used to represent the electron–ion interaction, removing core electrons from the explicit calculation (semi-core states can be retained where needed).
3. **A real-space grid** — used to represent the charge density and potentials, and to evaluate Hamiltonian matrix elements that cannot be reduced to two-center integrals (e.g., the exchange–correlation and Hartree terms).

The combination of strictly localized orbitals and pseudopotentials with finite support allows the Hamiltonian and overlap matrices to be computed with a cost that scales **linearly** with the number of atoms, and — with an appropriate choice of solver — the full self-consistent-field (SCF) cycle can also be made to scale linearly (order-*N*), which was historically SIESTA's headline achievement: the first self-consistent, linear-scaling implementation of DFT.

### 2.2 Basis set — Numerical Atomic Orbitals (NAOs / PAOs)

- Each orbital is the product of a numerically tabulated **radial function** and a **real spherical harmonic**.
- Radial functions are typically generated as the confined eigenfunctions of an isolated pseudo-atom subject to a soft-confinement potential (pseudo-atomic orbitals, PAOs), following the scheme introduced by Sankey and Niklewski and later refined by the SIESTA developers.
- The basis is **unconstrained in cardinality**: arbitrary numbers of zetas (multiple-ζ), angular momenta, polarization orbitals, and off-site (bond-centered / "floating") orbitals can be used.
- Typical production-quality calculations use **10–20 orbitals per atom** (e.g., a double-ζ polarized, DZP, basis), compared with several hundred plane waves per atom in typical plane-wave codes — a major source of SIESTA's efficiency, particularly for systems with large vacuum regions (surfaces, slabs, 0D/1D/2D nanostructures), since empty space costs essentially nothing in a localized basis.
- Because convergence with an atomic-orbital basis is not governed by a single monotonic parameter (unlike a plane-wave cutoff), basis-set optimization is a well-known and explicit responsibility of the user; SIESTA provides tools to assist in this (e.g., systematic soft-confinement / energy-shift schemes, basis optimization utilities). Careful DZP-type bases have been shown to reproduce well-converged plane-wave results to within ~10 μeV/atom in benchmark systems (e.g., the water dimer binding energy).

### 2.3 Pseudopotentials

- SIESTA uses **norm-conserving pseudopotentials**, historically in Troullier–Martins form, factorized in fully separable Kleinman–Bylander projector form.
- **PSML (PSeudopotential Markup Language)** is a modern, code-agnostic XML-based format co-developed by the SIESTA team to standardize pseudopotential data and metadata, improving interoperability across codes. PSML files can be generated by the ONCVPSP and ATOM generator programs and are supported by the curated **Pseudo-Dojo** database.
- The `libPSML` library allows any interested electronic-structure code (SIESTA, ABINIT, and others) to read PSML data transparently.
- Semi-core states can be included explicitly in the valence when core–valence overlap or transferability considerations require it.
- Non-linear core corrections (partial-core corrections) are supported.

### 2.4 The real-space grid

- Serves as the reciprocal-space analogue of a plane-wave basis: its fineness is parametrized by an equivalent kinetic-energy (mesh) cutoff, directly comparable to the plane-wave "density cutoff."
- Used to solve the Poisson equation for the Hartree potential (via FFT), and to compute matrix elements of terms (Hartree, exchange–correlation) that involve more than two centers and therefore cannot be pre-tabulated as two-center integrals.
- Implies periodic boundary conditions by default; non-periodic systems (molecules, wires, slabs) are treated with supercells, although alternative Poisson solvers (see §3) now allow open and mixed boundary conditions directly.
- Introduces a known discretization artifact, the "egg-box effect" (spurious energy variation as atoms move relative to the fixed grid), which is mitigated by adequate mesh-cutoff convergence and grid-cell filtering techniques.

### 2.5 Solving the Kohn–Sham problem: from cubic to linear scaling

The default and most universally applicable solution method is **direct diagonalization** of the generalized eigenvalue problem *H*Ψ = *ε*SΨ (via LAPACK/ScaLAPACK, or more efficient drop-in alternatives such as ELPA), which formally scales as O(*N*³) but benefits from the very small basis cardinality and highly efficient Hamiltonian construction.

For larger systems, SIESTA offers multiple **reduced- and linear-scaling solvers**, exploiting the sparsity of *H* and *S* that follows directly from the finite support of the NAO basis (Kohn's "nearsightedness" principle):

- **Original order-N functional minimization** (Kim–Mauri–Galli–type functional), avoiding explicit orthogonalization, with additional localization constraints on the density-matrix.
- **Orbital Minimization Method (OMM)** — a variational, cubic-scaling-but-competitive method (linear-scaling when combined with localization constraints) that can reuse data across SCF iterations.
- **Divide-and-conquer (D&C)** algorithms.
- **Fermi Operator Expansion (FOE)**, via the CheSS library (from the BigDFT project).
- **PEXSI** (Pole EXpansion and Selected Inversion) — reduces the effective cost of computing selected elements of the density matrix to at most O(*N*²) for dense/2D systems and O(*N*) for quasi-1D systems, with excellent parallel scalability over poles; used, e.g., to compute an 11,700-atom graphene nanoflake.
- **NTPoly** — density-matrix purification methods.
- Access to essentially all of the above (plus EigenExa, MAGMA, SLEPc) is unified through the **ELSI** (ELectronic Structure Infrastructure) interface, which SIESTA helped shape and now uses as its primary route to advanced solvers, including mixed-precision and GPU-accelerated modes (via ELPA/MAGMA) and multi-level (k-point + spin + solver) parallelization.

## 3. Key Physical and Computational Capabilities

| Category | Capabilities |
|---|---|
| **Exchange–correlation** | LDA, GGA (via internal implementation and via LibXC), meta-GGA support, screened hybrid functionals (HSE06, using Gaussian-fitted NAOs and the LIBINT library for four-center ERIs), efficient non-local van der Waals (vdW-DF-type) functionals with O(*N* log *N*) scaling |
| **Strong correlation** | DFT+U (Dudarev's rotationally invariant simplified formulation) |
| **Relativistic effects** | Full (non-perturbative) spin-orbit coupling, via both an *on-site* approximation and a fully separable *off-site* (Hemstreet-type) fully relativistic pseudopotential formalism; non-collinear magnetism |
| **Structural relaxation & dynamics** | Geometry optimization (conjugate gradients, (L)BFGS, FIRE, etc.), Born–Oppenheimer molecular dynamics, various thermostats/barostats, nudged elastic band (NEB) and variants (DNEB, VC-NEB) for transition-state search via the Lua/FLOS scripting layer |
| **Vibrational properties** | Force-constant (Vibra) and Density Functional Perturbation Theory (DFPT, formerly the separate LINRES code, now merged into the SIESTA trunk) approaches to Γ-point phonons; thermal transport via the approach-to-equilibrium molecular dynamics (AEMD) method |
| **Electronic transport** | TranSIESTA — non-equilibrium Green's function (NEGF) ballistic transport with multi-electrode (N≥1) support, real-space self-energies, block-tri-diagonal/MUMPS/dense inversion algorithms, thermoelectric calculations; TBtrans for post-processing and tight-binding transport; phonon transport (PHtrans) and inelastic transport (Inelastica) |
| **Time-dependent phenomena** | Real-time propagating TD-DFT (Crank–Nicolson-based propagators, including extrapolated and two-step schemes for strongly perturbed systems), applied to optical spectra and electronic stopping of fast projectiles; linear-response TD-DFT via companion open-source tools (PySCF-NAO, etc.) built on SIESTA orbitals |
| **Core-level spectroscopy** | Initial-state and final-state (core-hole) approaches to core-level binding-energy shifts |
| **Wannierization** | Native library-level interface to Wannier90 (maximally localized Wannier functions), enabling multiscale ("second-principles") modeling |
| **Multiscale modeling** | Second-principles DFT (SPDFT) via the companion SCALE-UP code, parameterized from SIESTA-computed force fields and Wannier Hamiltonians, for simulations of hundreds of thousands of atoms |
| **Analysis tools** | Density of states (total/partial), Mulliken and other population analyses, COOP/COHP bonding analysis, "fat bands," band unfolding for supercells, spin-texture calculation, STM image simulation |
| **Scripting / automation** | Embedded Lua interpreter (via the FLOOK library) for non-intrusive extension of program flow (custom MD integrators, relaxation algorithms, SCF-mixing schemes) without recompilation |
| **Workflow integration** | Official AiiDA plugin (`aiida-siesta`) for provenance-tracked, high-throughput workflows |
| **Dimensionality** | Handles 0D (clusters, molecules), 1D (wires, nanotubes, chains), 2D (slabs, monolayers), and 3D (bulk) systems natively, with vacuum regions costing negligible extra basis-set overhead |

## 4. Software Architecture and Ecosystem

- **Parallelization:** Hybrid MPI/OpenMP, with multiple layers of parallelism (over orbitals/atoms, k-points, spins, and — for pole-based solvers — over poles/energy points).
- **External libraries:** BLAS, LAPACK, ScaLAPACK, NetCDF/HDF5 (I/O), and optionally ELSI, ELPA, PEXSI, LibXC, LIBINT (for hybrid functionals), MUMPS (for TranSIESTA), and the embedded Lua interpreter.
- **Electronic Structure Library (ESL) participation:** several SIESTA-originated modules have been spun off as standalone, reusable libraries, including `libGridXC` (exchange-correlation on a grid), `libPSML` (PSML pseudopotential handling), and `xmlf90` (general XML handling); conversely SIESTA consumes ESL-hosted libraries such as ELSI.
- **Companion / satellite tools:**
  - **TranSIESTA / TBtrans / Inelastica / PHtrans** — quantum transport suite.
  - **sisl** — a general Python toolbox (originally SIESTA/TranSIESTA-specific, now broader) for post-processing Hamiltonians, density matrices, real-space grid data, and for building custom tight-binding models; interoperates with the Atomic Simulation Environment (ASE).
  - **SCALE-UP** — second-principles DFT multiscale engine.
  - **FLOS / FLOOK** — Lua-based scripting libraries for geometry relaxation and MD algorithm prototyping.
  - **aiida-siesta** — AiiDA plugin and workflow package.
  - Visualization/post-processing utilities for XCrySDen and VESTA (`xv2xsf`, `xv2vesta`, `vib2xsf`, `vib2vesta`), phonon analysis tools (`phdos`, `vibent`, `velcf`).
- **Distribution:** conda-forge packages available for Linux (x86-64, aarch64) and macOS (Intel, Apple Silicon); source builds via CMake/Meson-based build systems from GitLab.

## 5. Strengths and Limitations

**Strengths**
- Very small basis cardinality (≈10–20 orbitals/atom) gives high efficiency for systems with vacuum (surfaces, 2D materials, nanostructures) at essentially no extra basis-set cost.
- Genuine linear-scaling and reduced-scaling solver options (OMM, PEXSI, FOE/NTPoly via ELSI) make systems of many thousands to tens of thousands of atoms tractable.
- Atomic-orbital basis provides an intuitive, chemically transparent language (populations, COOP/COHP, Wannier functions) not naturally available in plane-wave codes.
- Broad physical scope: standard ground-state DFT, DFT+U, hybrid functionals, spin-orbit coupling, TD-DFT, DFPT phonons, and NEGF quantum transport within a single, integrated package.
- Fully open-source (GPLv3) since 2016, with an active GitLab-based development model, participation in EU infrastructure (MaX, ESL), and a large, long-standing user community across condensed-matter physics, materials science, nanoscience, and molecular biology.

**Limitations**
- Unlike a plane-wave cutoff, atomic-orbital basis-set convergence is not governed by a single, universally monotonic parameter; basis-set construction and validation require user expertise (a limitation shared with the broader quantum-chemistry LCAO community).
- Core electrons are removed via pseudopotentials, so all-electron/core-electron-sensitive properties require dedicated schemes (e.g., core-level shift approximations) rather than being directly accessible.
- The real-space grid formalism is intrinsically tied to a Fourier (FFT-based) Poisson solver and therefore to periodic boundary conditions by default, although alternative solvers now relax this constraint for isolated/mixed-dimensionality systems.
- As with all DFT-based codes, standard exchange-correlation functionals have well-known shortcomings for strongly correlated systems, only partly addressed via DFT+U and hybrid-functional extensions.
